### Email 5: Navigating Prior Authorizations

**Subject:** 73% of doctors say PAs delay care. Here's how to reduce them.

**Preview Text:** Strategic prescribing can minimize prior authorization headaches

**Email Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Let's talk about everyone's least favorite administrative burden: **prior authorizations**.

### The Prior Authorization Problem:

**Survey Data:**
- 73% of physicians report PAs delay necessary care
- Average time per PA: 16 minutes
- 91% of physicians report PAs sometimes, often, or always delay care
- 28% of PAs are never completed

**The human cost:**
Delayed treatment. Worsening conditions. Patient frustration. Physician burnout.

### Why PAs Exist:

Insurance companies use PAs to:
- Control costs on expensive medications
- Ensure appropriate medication use
- Require trial of generic alternatives first
- Manage specialty medication distribution

**Frustrating? Yes. Going away anytime soon? Probably not.**

### Strategic Prescribing to Reduce PAs:

**1. Know Which Medications Trigger PAs**

High-PA Categories:
- Brand medications with generic equivalents
- Specialty/biologic medications
- High-cost drugs (>$500/month)
- Controlled substances in some states
- Medications with "step therapy" requirements

**2. Prescribe Generics First (When Clinically Appropriate)**

Most PAs are for brand medications when generics exist. If therapeutic outcomes are equivalent, generic-first saves everyone time.

**3. Consider Compound Alternatives**

Compounded medications often don't require PA because:
- They're not standard formulary items
- They're custom-prepared for specific patients
- Insurance processes them differently

**Example:** Compounded bio-identical hormones typically don't require PA, while Premarin often does.

**4. Use Medications with Fewer PA Requirements**

Within a therapeutic class, some medications have fewer restrictions:
- Check your insurance formularies
- Track which meds consistently require PA
- Build your go-to list of low-PA alternatives

### When PA Is Unavoidable:

**Speed up the process:**
✓ Submit complete clinical documentation upfront
✓ Use electronic PA systems when available
✓ Have pharmacist submit PA (they're often faster)
✓ Appeal denials with evidence-based justification

**Or consider alternatives:**
✓ Therapeutic substitution to non-PA medication
✓ Compound pharmacy alternative
✓ Patient assistance programs
✓ Cash-pay pricing comparison

### The Cost-PA Tradeoff:

Sometimes a cash-pay compound medication is:
- Cheaper than the insurance copay for a brand drug
- Available immediately (no PA wait)
- Equally effective therapeutically

**Real example:**
Brand progesterone with insurance after PA: $120/month (2-week wait)
Compounded progesterone cash price: $45/month (available next day)

The patient often saves money AND time by skipping insurance entirely.

### eNavvi's PA Tools:

- PA likelihood indicators (shows which meds commonly require PA)
- Alternative suggestions (non-PA equivalents)
- Compound pharmacy options (often PA-free)
- Direct pharmacy communication (faster PA processing)

### The Bottom Line:

You can't eliminate PAs, but strategic prescribing can significantly reduce them—saving you time and getting patients treatment faster.

## SECTION 8: EMAIL BEST PRACTICES

### General Guidelines:

**Subject Lines:**
- Keep under 50 characters when possible
- Use actionable language
- Create curiosity without clickbait
- Include numbers when relevant ("5 ways...", "73% of doctors...")
- A/B test different approaches

**Preview Text:**
- Complement the subject line (don't repeat it)
- Provide additional context
- 40-100 characters optimal
- Make it scannable

**Email Body:**
- Professional, conversational tone
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
- Use bullet points and subheadings
- One clear CTA per email
- Mobile-responsive design

**Personalization:**
- Use recipient's name (first or last based on audience)
- Segment by specialty when possible
- Reference their practice type
- Acknowledge their prescribing patterns (when appropriate)

### Timing & Frequency:

**Clinician Emails:**
- Send Tuesday-Thursday for best open rates
- Avoid Monday mornings (inbox overload)
- Avoid late Friday (low engagement)
- Optimal time: 8-9am or 12-1pm (between patients)

**Patient Emails:**
- Send evenings or weekends for better engagement
- Tuesday and Thursday evenings perform best
- Avoid early mornings (rushed, low attention)

**Frequency:**
- Onboarding: Once every 3-7 days
- Newsletter: Monthly
- Feature announcements: As needed (no more than 2/month)
- Re-engagement: 30 days, 60 days, then quarterly

### Compliance & Legal:

**✓ Always Include:**
- Clear unsubscribe link
- Physical mailing address
- Privacy policy link
- HIPAA-compliant language (no PHI in email)

**✗ Never Include:**
- Medical advice
- Protected health information
- Unsupported medical claims
- Therapeutic promises

### A/B Testing Recommendations:

Test these variables:
- Subject line length and style
- Send time/day
- CTA button text
- Email length (short vs. comprehensive)
- Use of statistics vs. stories
- Professional vs. friendly tone

### Metrics to Track:

**Primary:**
- Open rate (target: 25-35% for clinicians, 18-25% for patients)
- Click-through rate (target: 3-8%)
- Conversion rate (sign-ups, webinar registrations, etc.)
- Unsubscribe rate (acceptable: <0.5%)

**Secondary:**
- Time to open after send
- Device used (mobile vs. desktop)
- Link popularity (which CTAs get clicked)
- Forward/share rate
