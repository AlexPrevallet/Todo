# eNavvi Brand Guidelines for Content Creation

## Brand Voice

eNavvi communicates with a voice that is:

### Professional yet Approachable
- Use clear, accessible language without being overly casual
- Maintain clinical credibility while being warm and human
- Balance expertise with empathy

**Examples:**
- ✅ "We're changing how clinicians prescribe by bringing transparency to the process"
- ❌ "We're totally revolutionizing healthcare!" (too casual/hype)
- ❌ "Our pharmaceutical distribution optimization platform leverages..." (too corporate)

### Evidence-Based and Trustworthy
- Back claims with data, statistics, or clinical evidence
- Use concrete numbers when possible
- Reference established medical standards (USP, FDA guidelines)
- Cite sources when making specific claims

**Examples:**
- ✅ "**73% of clinics** report GLP-1 shortages (Source: ASHP Drug Shortage Database)"
- ✅ "Compound pharmacies following USP <795> and <797> standards"
- ❌ "Many doctors use our platform" (vague, unsubstantiated)
- ❌ "The best prescribing solution available" (subjective, hype)

### Empowering for Healthcare Providers
- Position clinicians as decision-makers and experts
- Focus on tools that enhance their practice
- Respect their intelligence and expertise
- Avoid telling doctors what to do

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Empowering clinicians with the tools to prescribe confidently"
- ✅ "Supporting your clinical decision-making with transparent data"
- ❌ "You need to start prescribing this way" (prescriptive, condescending)

### Patient-Centric
- Ultimately, all value propositions should tie back to patient outcomes
- Emphasize access, affordability, and quality of care
- Focus on adherence, continuity of care, and patient satisfaction

**Examples:**
- ✅ "When patients know costs upfront, adherence increases"
- ✅ "Treatment continuity shouldn't depend on supply chain luck"
- ❌ "Save your practice money on prescriptions" (practice-focused, not patient-focused)

### Innovative and Forward-Thinking
- Acknowledge that healthcare is evolving
- Position eNavvi as part of the solution to modern challenges
- Don't disparage traditional methods, but offer better alternatives
- Future-oriented without being unrealistic

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Modern prescribing requires modern tools"
- ✅ "Adapting to healthcare's evolving challenges"
- ❌ "Old-school prescribing is dead" (alienating, divisive)

## Tone Guidelines

### DO:
✅ Use clinical terminology appropriately for HCP audiences
✅ Back claims with data and evidence
✅ Respect the intelligence of healthcare professionals
✅ Focus on patient outcomes and quality of care
✅ Be specific and concrete
✅ Use active voice
✅ Write in present tense when possible
✅ Include statistics formatted in bold: **73% of clinics**
✅ Use bullet points for easy scanning

### DON'T:
❌ Oversell or use hype language
❌ Make unsubstantiated claims
❌ Use excessive exclamation points
❌ Include medical jargon when targeting patients
❌ Be vague or use corporate speak
❌ Use patient testimonials on B2B platforms (LinkedIn)
❌ Oversimplify complex medical concepts
❌ Use excessive emojis (1-2 max, professional contexts only)
❌ Make direct comparisons to competitors
❌ Promise outcomes that can't be guaranteed

## Messaging Pillars

Every piece of content should align with at least one of these core messages:

### 1. Transparent Prescription Drug Pricing
**Message**: Patients and providers deserve to know medication costs before prescribing
**Key points**:
- Real-time pricing at point of prescribing
- Compare brand, generic, and compound options
- No surprise costs at pharmacy counter
- Improves patient adherence and satisfaction

**Example language**:
"What if your patients knew prescription costs before leaving your office? eNavvi brings real-time pricing transparency to your prescribing workflow."

### 2. Access to Quality Compound Pharmacy Network
**Message**: Compounding is essential medicine when commercial options don't fit
**Key points**:
- Vetted pharmacies with USP <795> and <797> compliance
- Quality-assured formulations
- Customization for specific patient needs
- Alternative to unavailable brand medications

**Example language**:
"Quality-assured compound pharmacy isn't alternative medicine—it's essential medicine for personalized patient care."

### 3. Physician-Friendly Prescribing Platform
**Message**: Free, easy-to-use platform that integrates with existing workflows
**Key points**:
- Zero fees for prescribers
- E-prescribing integration
- No disruption to existing practice management
- Clinical support and dosing protocols

**Example language**:
"Prescribe with confidence: eNavvi integrates seamlessly into your workflow at zero cost to your practice."

### 4. Cost-Effective Alternatives to Brand Medications
**Message**: Affordable options that don't compromise on quality
**Key points**:
- Significant cost savings (be specific with numbers)
- Same molecular structure as brand versions
- Improves patient adherence through affordability
- Insurance-independent options

**Example language**:
"**$1,200+/month** for brand semaglutide vs. affordable compound alternatives. The math matters for patient adherence."

### 5. Solutions for Drug Shortages
**Message**: Maintain treatment continuity even when supply chains fail
**Key points**:
- Address ongoing pharmaceutical shortages
- Compound alternatives during brand unavailability
- Clinical continuity of care
- Proactive shortage planning

**Example language**:
"**300+ medications** are currently in shortage. Your prescribing platform should be part of the solution, not another obstacle."

### 6. Personalized Medicine Through Compounding
**Message**: One-size-fits-all doesn't work for every patient
**Key points**:
- Custom dosing for pediatric/geriatric patients
- Alternative delivery methods (topical, sublingual, etc.)
- Allergen-free formulations
- Combination therapies for better adherence

**Example language**:
"When FDA-approved options don't fit your patient's unique needs, compound pharmacy becomes clinical necessity, not convenience."

## Writing Style

### Sentence Structure
- Vary sentence length for readability
- Use short sentences for emphasis
- Break up long paragraphs with bullet points
- Start with a strong hook

### Word Choice
- Active voice: "eNavvi provides" not "is provided by eNavvi"
- Present tense: "eNavvi connects" not "eNavvi will connect"
- Concrete nouns: "clinicians" not "healthcare stakeholders"
- Strong verbs: "empowering" not "helping"

### Formatting
- **Bold** key statistics and numbers
- Use bullet points (•) for lists
- Line breaks for readability
- Hashtags at the end (3-5 for LinkedIn)

### Statistics and Data
- Always bold: **73%**, **$1,200+/month**, **300+ medications**
- Round numbers for readability
- Include context: "**73% of clinics** report..." not just "**73%** of respondents..."
- Source when possible (in longer content)

## Content Types by Platform

### LinkedIn
- **Length**: 150-250 words
- **Format**: Professional, data-driven
- **Hook**: Statistic, question, or industry pain point
- **Body**: Clinical value, evidence, specific applications
- **CTA**: Professional, educational (not sales-y)

- **Length**: 200-280 characters (leave room for engagement)
- **Format**: Concise, impactful
- **Hook**: Bold statement or statistic

- **Tone**: Professional but approachable, shareable

- **Length**: 150-300 words
- **Format**: Visual-first, story-driven
- **Hook**: Relatable scenario or patient-centric statement
- **Body**: Simplified benefits, community focus
- **CTA**: "Link in bio" or "Learn more"
- **Hashtags**: 5-10 community tags
- **Tone**: Warm, accessible, community-oriented

## Legal and Compliance

### Always Avoid:
❌ Unverified medical claims
❌ Patient testimonials without proper consent
❌ Direct comparisons to specific competitor products
❌ Promises of specific health outcomes
❌ Off-label use suggestions without proper context
❌ Price guarantees (use "starting at" or "as low as")

### Always Include:
✅ Disclaimers when appropriate
✅ References to FDA/USP standards when discussing quality
✅ "Consult your healthcare provider" language for patient content
✅ Sources for statistics and data points
✅ Compliance review approval before posting

### HIPAA Considerations:
- Never use identifiable patient information
- Stock photos only (no actual patients without consent)
- Generic clinical scenarios only
- No protected health information in any form
